🔬 Molecular Biomarkers in Neuroinflammation

13 biomarkers in CSF and blood: cytokines, structural proteins and glial damage markers — AD vs PD vs MS vs Controls

🏦 Harvard DataverseDOI: 10.7910/DVN/X2TQQA 13 biomarkers3 diseases
Summary: Molecular biomarkers in cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) and blood are essential for early diagnosis, progression monitoring and treatment evaluation in neurodegenerative diseases. This analysis compares 13 key biomarkers in patients with Alzheimer's, Parkinson's and Multiple Sclerosis against healthy controls. Neurofilament light chain (NfL) is the most sensitive yet non-specific neuronal damage biomarker; p-Tau 181 is highly specific for AD; IFN-γ is proportionally most elevated in MS.

Findings

Inflammatory biomarkers show non-specific elevations in all three diseases, but with differentiated patterns. AD: p-Tau and Aβ42 are the most specific (ratio 4.3x and 0.4x respectively). PD: α-synuclein in CSF shows an inverse pattern — reduction, not elevation — because it accumulates in Lewy bodies within neural tissue. MS: IFN-γ (24.8x) reflects the T-lymphocyte-mediated autoimmune nature of the disease. Plasma NfL is the universal biomarker of active neurodegeneration with the greatest utility in treatment monitoring.
