Summary: Neuroimaging provides critical structural and functional biomarkers for the diagnosis and monitoring of neurodegenerative diseases. Amyloid PET (85% positive in AD) enables diagnosis 10–15 years before symptom onset. DAT-SPECT (75% deficit in PD) quantifies dopaminergic loss. T2-FLAIR lesions on MRI (average 18 in MS) are the diagnostic gold standard for MS. Hippocampal atrophy (35% in AD) correlates directly with cognitive decline.

Findings

Each neurodegenerative disease has a characteristic neuroimaging profile: AD is distinguished by severe hippocampal atrophy (35%) and high amyloid PET positivity (85%), reflecting Aβ deposition. PD stands out for its DAT-SPECT deficit (75%), specific for the loss of dopaminergic neurons in the substantia nigra. MS is unique for its multiple T2-FLAIR lesions (average 18), reflecting multifocal demyelination. FDG-PET hypometabolism is the least specific parameter but useful for functional follow-up in all three diseases.
